How 11 Top Agencies Got Their First Clients, and What Pays Most

Ogilvy, Infosys, 37signals, Designjoy, Schbang and more: how solo founders, duos and teams won first clients, and which agency services earn the most.

Short answer

Most great agencies started with one or two people, no clients and very little money. They got their first clients from proof they had already made (a blog, their own brand, a demo), not from pitching. They grew by narrowing to one offer billed every month, and many of the biggest outcomes came from turning a service into a product. On services: development brings the biggest revenue but the thinnest margin, design and small studios keep the most profit, SEO and paid ads bring recurring revenue, and AI integration is where demand is growing fastest.

Key takeaways
  • Proof before pitch. Vayner, Moz, 37signals and Designjoy all won clients with work people could see before they asked.
  • One narrow offer beats a menu. Specialist agencies grew about 13% in 2025 against a 7.5% average, per Promethean Research data.
  • Small is more profitable; big grows faster. Studios under 10 people kept ~19% net margin; agencies of 50+ kept ~8%.
  • Revenue and profit live in different services. Development agencies bill the most and keep the least.
  • The big exits came from products. Basecamp, Moz and Tiny all began as client-service shops.

This is a field guide, not a list of famous names. For each agency we looked for four things: how it started, where the first clients came from, the moment it nearly broke, and what it turned into. Then we put the services side by side to see which ones actually pay.

Every number below links to its source. Figures were checked on 23 November 2026. Where a number is self-reported by the founder or comes from a secondary summary, we say so.

Solo, duo or team: which start works?

All three work. They fail in different ways, so pick the one whose weakness you can live with.

Solo: fast, cheap, capped by your hours

MetaLab was one person cold-emailing startup founders10. Andrew Wilkinson presented it as a bigger agency and booked about $15,000 of work in his first month25. Designjoy is still one person. Brett Williams said he made over $50,000 a month alone, working 50 to 60 hours a week, with about $2,000 a year in costs15.

The catch

Solo works when the work is packaged so tightly that one person can deliver it. Designjoy takes unlimited requests but works on one at a time16. That one rule is what makes "unlimited" survivable.

Duo: one sells, one delivers

The pattern repeats: VaynerMedia (brothers Gary and AJ), Moz (Rand Fishkin and his mother Gillian), Social Beat (brothers Vikas and Suneil Chawla), Webchutney (Sidharth Rao and Sudesh Samaria), Directive (Garrett Mehrguth and Tanner Shaffer). Usually one partner is the public face and the other runs operations. AJ Vaynerchuk, for example, covered finance, operations and client service in the early days13.

Team: slower start, longer runway

Infosys began with seven engineers and ₹10,0004. Sudha Murty later said getting the very first customer was extremely hard5. A team shares the pain of a slow start; a solo founder has to absorb it alone.

Where did the first clients actually come from?

Not from pitching strangers with a deck. In almost every case, the founder had already made something a buyer could see.

  • Their own brand's results. Gary Vaynerchuk built his family's Wine Library online, then sold the same playbook to other brands12.
  • A blog. Rand Fishkin started blogging what he learned about SEO. Clients began asking for SEO instead of web design8.
  • A public point of view. 37signals launched with a manifesto on how the web should be designed. The New York Times wrote about it26.
  • Social posts. Brett Williams has said Twitter was Designjoy's main lead source27.
  • Cheap, small jobs. Directive's founder bid on Fiverr gigs and lived on about $12 a day while he learned search17.
What this means for you

Before you send 100 cold emails, make one thing a buyer can check in two minutes: a before/after for a real business, a short teardown of their funnel, or a working demo. Cold outreach that links to proof is a different message from cold outreach that asks for a call.

What nearly killed them?

Every story has a moment that could have ended it. They are useful because they are specific.

  • Debt from bad delivery. Moz's founders were around $500,000 in personal debt, partly from hiring contractors who did poor work928.
  • One big deal falling through. Webchutney hit serious money trouble in 2007 when a deal with MakeMyTrip didn't happen20.
  • Founder dependence. Digiday reported that clients often came to VaynerMedia for Gary himself, which is hard to scale29.

The lesson is boring and true: one client should not be able to sink you, and delivery quality is a cash-flow issue, not just a reputation one.

How did they grow from first client to real company?

1. They narrowed the offer

Directive sells only annual contracts, with a minimum of about $10,000 a month, to B2B software companies18. Designjoy sells one plan16. Promethean Research's 2026 data, as summarised by Agiled, shows specialist agencies grew about 13% in 2025, almost double the 7.5% average31.

2. They moved to monthly billing

A project pays once. A retainer or subscription pays every month. Designjoy's whole model is a monthly subscription16, and 78% of SEO providers in Ahrefs' survey bill a monthly retainer32.

3. They grew with the channel, not against it

Webchutney started in 1999, when brands spent almost nothing on digital39. VaynerMedia bet on social media in 200912. Social Beat began as a social media company, then added ads and SEO as clients needed them23, and went from ₹7 crore revenue in 2019 to a ₹40 crore run rate in FY2223.

4. They tied fees to client results

Gary Vaynerchuk told Business Insider he actively seeks pay linked to how much the client sells14. Directive's site now calls it a "revenue partner" rather than an agency30.

5. Some turned the service into a product

This is where the biggest outcomes came from.

  • 37signals built Basecamp to manage its own client projects. It reached $5,000 a month within weeks. A year or so later it earned more than web design, so they stopped doing web design6.
  • Moz launched its first subscription tool at $39 a month while still consulting, then raised $1.1M in 20079.
  • MetaLab's profits funded Tiny's first acquisitions11.
  • Social Beat spun out Influencer.in and D2Scale33.

Notice the order. None of them started as a product company. The client work showed them which problem was worth a product.

Is a small agency more profitable than a big one?

Usually, yes. But it grows more slowly. That trade-off is the most useful thing a founder can know before deciding to hire.

Across agencies, Promethean Research puts the average net margin at about 13% in 2025, even though the average project margin was about 35%34. The 22 points in between are salaries for people who aren't billing, tools, rent and unpaid pitches.

The non-obvious part

  • Revenue and profit sit in different places. Development brings the biggest bills and the thinnest margin34. If you need cash flow, build. If you need profit, design or advise.
  • Ads income depends on the client's budget. With a %-of-spend fee, your revenue rises when they spend more and falls the month they cut back35.
  • SEO prices are not global. India-based SEOs charge far less than US ones in Ahrefs' survey32. An Indian agency serving overseas clients earns the gap; one serving only local clients competes on price.
  • AI demand is "AI applied to a job", not "AI in general". Upwork's research lead described the growth as AI used within a context37. "AI chatbot for admission enquiries" sells; "AI services" doesn't.
  • The best package is a bundle around one outcome. Build the automation (project cash), run it monthly (recurring), and feed it with ads or search (demand). Each service covers the others' weakness.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take an agency to go from 0 to 1?

It varies a lot. Basecamp beat 37signals' design revenue about a year after launch. Designjoy ran as a side project for years. Moz took years to get out of debt. Plan for slow, and count months to the first repeat client, not the first client.

Should I start an agency alone or with a co-founder?

Alone is fastest and keeps the most margin, but your hours cap it. Many agencies in this list started as duos: one sells, one delivers. Choose the weakness you can live with.

Which agency service is most profitable?

In Promethean Research's 2026 data, design agencies had the highest net margin (about 18%) and development agencies the lowest (about 11%). Studios under 10 people averaged about 19%.

Which agency service is most in demand in 2026?

AI applied to a specific job. On Upwork, AI integration demand rose 178% and AI chatbot development 71% in 2025. Established skills like full-stack development stayed strong.

Retainer or project pricing: which is better?

Projects bring cash; retainers bring predictability. Most SEO providers (78% in Ahrefs' survey) bill retainers. A common path is a paid build first, then a monthly fee to run it.

What should you do today?

Write your offer in one line, the way Designjoy does: who it is for, the one result, the monthly price, and how a client can stop. If it doesn't fit in one line, it is still a menu.
