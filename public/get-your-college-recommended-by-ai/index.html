How a College Gets Recommended by ChatGPT and Google AI

What AI assistants look at before they name a college, what Google and OpenAI say, and a seven-step checklist any college can do this month.

Key takeaways
  • Google says there is no special trick for its AI answers. A page needs to be indexed and allowed to show a snippet, like any other page.
  • ChatGPT search can show any public site, but only if you don't block its crawler, OAI-SearchBot. Nobody can promise inclusion.
  • The one academic test of "AI visibility" found that adding statistics, quotes and cited sources helped, and stuffing keywords did worse than doing nothing.
  • Seven steps below. A college can do all of them in a month without paying for anything.

A parent types "best BBA college in Mangalore with good placements" into ChatGPT, Gemini or Google. The assistant names three colleges. Yours is either one of them or it isn't. This post is about what you can control.

What happens when a parent asks an AI

For a question like this, assistants with web search usually don't answer from memory. They search the web, read a handful of pages and write an answer from them. So you win or lose at the two middle steps: can it read your pages, and are they worth quoting?

Step 1: Let the crawlers in

Google's guidance says a page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet to appear in its AI answers, and that Google adds no other technical requirement. OpenAI's says any public website can appear in ChatGPT search, but your site must not block its crawler, OAI-SearchBot.

  • Open yourcollege.edu.in/robots.txt and check that nothing blocks Googlebot or OAI-SearchBot.
  • If your site sits behind a firewall or CDN, check it isn't blocking them either.
  • Verify your site in Google Search Console and submit the sitemap.

Step 2: Put the answers in plain text

Parents ask the same things: fees, eligibility, duration, placements, approvals, location, how to apply. If those answers live only in a PDF, an image or a pop-up, the assistant can't quote them. Give each course its own page with those answers as text, under headings that match the question.

Example (made up)

Heading: "What is the eligibility for BBA at Your College?" Then, straight away, one or two sentences: "Students need a pass in Class 12 from a recognised board. Admission is on merit." The answer comes first and the detail follows. That shape is easy to quote.

Step 3: Add facts, sources and named voices

In 2023, a team of university researchers tested ways to make content show up more in AI answers. They reported that adding statistics, adding quotations and citing sources raised visibility by up to about 40% in their tests. Keyword stuffing scored below doing nothing.

  • Use real numbers you can back up, such as batch size, recruiters visited or years of approval, and say where they come from.
  • Quote a named person, such as the head of placements or a faculty member.
  • Link to the official source for approvals and rankings.
  • Don't repeat "best BBA college in Mangalore" ten times.
Watch out

That study used a simulated search engine and, in a second check, Perplexity. It shows what tends to help, not what ChatGPT or Gemini will do for your college.

Step 4: Keep the same facts everywhere

Fees, address, phone number, course names and approvals should match on your website, Google Business Profile, social pages and directories. Google also lists keeping Business Profile details current as good practice. When sources disagree, an assistant has less reason to trust any of them.

Step 5: Add structured data that matches the page

Structured data is code that tells machines what a page is about, such as a college, a course or a list of questions. Google says it doesn't require special markup for its AI features, but any markup you add must match the visible text. So add it, and never put a fact in the code that isn't on the page.

Step 6: Get mentioned by other trusted sites

This one is my own read, not something I can source. Assistants seem to repeat what several sites say, so a mention on an education directory, a news story or an alumni page likely helps more than another page on your own site. I can't tell you how much weight it has.

Step 7: Check what the assistants actually say

  • Once a month, ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity ten questions a parent would ask. Write down which colleges appear and which pages they cite.
  • OpenAI says visits from ChatGPT search carry utm_source=chatgpt.com, so you can see them in Google Analytics.
  • Watch Search Console for impressions and clicks on your course pages.

What nobody can promise

No agency, including us, can guarantee that an assistant will name your college. Answers change by person, day and wording. Google also says changes to your pages can take days to months to be recrawled and reflected. And Google says you don't need new "AI files" such as an llms.txt. We publish one on this site as a low-cost extra, but I wouldn't count on it.

Do this today

Write down the ten questions parents ask before they apply. Ask them in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, and note whether your college appears. That list is your to-do list for the month.
