Only 5 Ways to Get Customers? For Agencies, Not Quite

The 5-ways rule (outreach, content, ads, partners, referrals) is a good start. Here's what agencies need on top, backed by agency survey data.

Short answer

Mostly true. Every customer finds you because you reached out, your content pulled them in, you paid for attention, a partner sent them, or a customer told them. But for an agency, the list hides things that need their own plan: warm vs cold outreach, being listed where buyers already shop, showing up when they search Google or ask AI, and events. And the cheapest growth is often a current client buying more.

Key takeaways
  • The "5 ways" line compresses Alex Hormozi's Core Four and his "lead getters" into one list. It's a good map of who does the work.
  • Agencies still get most new business from referrals: 66% named past and current clients as their top source in SparkToro's survey.
  • What the 5 ways miss is demand that already exists: directories, marketplaces, search and AI answers, tenders.
  • Plan each channel separately. "Reach out" to a friend and to a stranger are different jobs with different scripts.
  • Start by asking where your last 10 clients came from. Double the best source, then add one new one.

Where the "5 ways" comes from

The line goes around as a short clip: reach out, make content, run ads, get partners, let customers tell customers. It's a shortened version of Alex Hormozi's framework from his book $100M Leads.

Hormozi splits it into two parts. The Core Four are things you do yourself: warm outreach, free content, cold outreach and paid ads. The lead getters are other people who do the Core Four for you: customers (referrals), employees, agencies and affiliates.

So the clip merges warm and cold outreach into one, and drops employees and agencies. That's fine for a one-line summary. For planning, the missing pieces matter.

What agencies actually get clients from

Surveys agree on one thing: referrals win. In SparkToro and Paddy Moogan's survey of 612 agency owners and consultants, 66% said existing and past clients were their top source of new business.

The 2025 edition found the same. The top two drivers were referrals from clients and from partner companies (15% named partners first), and content and speaking at events made the top five.

A different lens: Redline Digital read about 4,000 client reviews on Clutch and found 22 distinct ways clients found their agency. Comparison sites like Clutch were about 6% of mentions, freelance platforms about 1%, events about 1%.

Read the numbers carefully

These are agencies in the US and Europe, mostly self-reported. A new agency with no past clients can't lean on referrals yet. It has to create the first ones through outreach and proof.

Warm and cold outreach are two different channels

"Reach out to people" sounds like one job. It's two. Warm outreach goes to people who already know you: classmates, past colleagues, alumni groups, old clients. Cold outreach goes to strangers.

Warm is where most first clients come from, because trust already exists. Cold is how you scale once warm runs out. The scripts, volumes and reply rates are completely different.

Example

Warm: a message to 50 alumni saying what you now help businesses with, and asking who they know that has the problem. Cold: 50 emails a day to clinic owners running Google Ads, each offering a free audit of their enquiry follow-up.

Be listed where buyers already shop

Some buyers have already decided to hire. They open Clutch, Upwork, Google Maps or JustDial and compare. None of the 5 ways covers this well, because you're not attracting them: you're making sure you're on the shelf when they walk in.

The cost is competition. Everyone on the page is one click away, so profiles with reviews, a narrow niche and a clear price win. On Upwork, applying to projects costs paid "Connects", so treat it like an ad budget, not a free channel.

Be the answer when they search or ask AI

Content and search look the same but do different jobs. Content pulls people in who weren't looking. Search catches people who are, at the moment they type "AI automation agency Bangalore" or ask ChatGPT for a recommendation.

That second job is SEO (ranking on Google), plus GEO and AEO (getting named in AI answers). We wrote up how it works for colleges in how to get your college recommended by AI; the same rules apply to agencies.

Events, workshops and tenders

Events are content delivered in a room: a talk, a workshop, a stall at an industry meetup. One talk to 40 business owners can do the work of 400 cold emails, because the trust is built live.

Tenders and RFPs matter if you sell to government, universities or large companies. The buyer publishes the need and picks from bids. It's slow and paperwork-heavy, but the buyer is real and the budget is set.

The growth that isn't a new customer

The "5 ways" are about new customers. But revenue also grows when a current client buys more: a website client who adds ads management, or a chatbot client who adds WhatsApp follow-ups. It's the cheapest sale, because the trust and the payment setup already exist.

Frequently asked questions

Are there really only 5 ways to get customers?

As a top-level map, mostly yes. In practice, warm and cold outreach, directories, search and AI answers, events and tenders each need their own plan, so it's more useful to plan around 8 to 10 channels.

What is the best way for a new agency to get its first clients?

Warm outreach to people who already know you, plus one piece of proof (a demo, a case study or a free audit). Referrals can't start until you have clients.

Where do established agencies get most new business?

Referrals from existing and past clients. In SparkToro's survey, 66% of agencies named it their top source, with partner referrals second.

Should an agency use Upwork or Clutch?

Yes if your buyers shop there and you can stand out with reviews and a narrow niche. Budget for it like an ad channel, since applying and premium listings cost money.

What should you do today?

Write down your last 10 clients and where each one came from. Put more time into the top source this month, and add exactly one channel from group 3.
