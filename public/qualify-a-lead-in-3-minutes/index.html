How to Qualify a Lead in 3 Minutes With 3 Questions

Three questions that qualify a sales lead in the first three minutes without sounding like an interview, and the answers that tell you to walk away.

Key takeaways
  • You can tell whether a lead is real in about three minutes with three questions, asked in order.
  • Start with today, not goals. "How are you handling this right now?" shows whether they have a process, a competitor or nothing.
  • Ask what is broken, not whether something is. "Nothing really" means no pain. Disqualify politely and move on.
  • Ask what it costs to stay broken. A number means urgency. No answer means they're not buying soon.

Most first sales calls go wrong in one of two ways. You pitch before you know if they need you, or you fire a checklist of questions (budget? authority? timeline?) and the call starts to feel like a job interview.

There's a quicker way. Three plain questions, each building on the last answer, tell you whether this person has a problem worth solving and a reason to solve it now. The other person just feels like you're curious about their business.

1. Ask about today, not their goals

Open with how things work right now:

  • "What are you using right now for this?"
  • "How are you handling this today?"

Goals are easy to talk about and cost nothing to say ("we want to grow 2x"). The current setup is a fact. The answer puts them in one of three buckets:

They sayWhat it means
"We have a process" (a spreadsheet, a person, a routine)They care enough to have built something. Look for the cracks in it.
"We use X" (a competitor or a tool)They already pay to solve this. Find what X doesn't do for them.
"Nothing, really"A fresh problem, or no problem at all. Question 2 tells you which.
Example

You sell enquiry follow-up for coaching institutes. You ask: "How are you handling enquiries today?" The owner says: "Two people on the front desk call back everyone from the website form and Instagram." Now you know there's a manual process, and you know where to look next.

2. Ask what's broken, not whether anything is

The wording matters. "Is anything not working?" invites a polite "no, it's fine". Ask it as if something obviously is:

  • "What's not working about that setup?"

Now they have to name something. Most people with a real problem will start talking straight away, and that's your pain point in their own words.

If the answer is "nothing really", believe them. You're talking to someone with no pain. Don't push. Thank them, leave the door open, and move on to the next lead. Your time is better spent there.

Example

Same owner: "Honestly, weekend enquiries wait till Monday, and by then half of them have joined somewhere else." That's a specific, real pain.

3. Ask what it costs if it stays broken

Pain alone doesn't mean they'll buy. Plenty of people live with a problem for years. The last question checks for urgency:

  • "If this continues for another 6 months, what does that cost you?"

Listen for a number. "We lose maybe 20 admissions a term" or "about two lakh a month" means they've already felt the cost. That's a real lead. If they can't answer, or shrug it off, there's no urgency yet. They may buy one day, just not this quarter.

Why it doesn't feel like an interview

  • Each question follows from their last answer. It sounds like a conversation, not a form.
  • They do the talking. You learn the problem and the cost in their words, which is exactly what you'll repeat back in your proposal.
  • No awkward money question. You never ask "what's your budget?". Question 3 tells you what the problem is worth to them, which matters more.
Watch out

Don't rush through all three to hit a script. If question 2 opens up a long answer, let it run. And don't argue with "nothing really". Talking someone into a problem they don't feel rarely ends in a sale that sticks.

Try it on your next call

Write the three questions on a sticky note, in order, and use them in the first three minutes of your next discovery call. After the call, mark the lead: real (gave a number), later (pain but no urgency) or no (no pain). After ten calls you'll see which bucket most of your leads fall into.
