What You Can Build With Meta for Developers (2026)

WhatsApp, Instagram, Threads, ads, AI and glasses: what Meta for Developers lets a business build in 2026, what it costs and which approvals you need.

Short answer

Meta for Developers lets you plug your own software into WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, Messenger, Threads and Meta ads, and build on Meta's AI models, AI glasses and VR. For most businesses the useful part is narrow: automate WhatsApp and Instagram conversations, and send lead and sale data back to Meta ads so they find better customers.

Key takeaways
  • One Meta app, one verified business and App Review unlock almost everything. Plan for the approvals, not just the code.
  • WhatsApp Cloud API is the biggest win for Indian businesses: replies, reminders, bots and calls on the number customers already use.
  • Lead webhooks plus the Conversions API close the loop between an ad, your CRM and a booked appointment.
  • WhatsApp pricing changed again on 1 October 2026: replies to customers now cost money after a free monthly allowance.
  • AI models, glasses and VR are real platforms now, but they matter for product companies, not for most local businesses yet.

Checked 23 November 2026 against Meta's developer documentation and blog. Meta changes these products often; where I rely on a third party for a date or price, the source says so.

What is Meta for Developers?

It is Meta's portal for anyone who wants software to talk to Meta's products. You create an app (a set of keys and permissions, not something people download), connect it to your business portfolio, and switch on the products you need. [1]

The site is split into developer centres: social technologies (Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp, Threads, ads), AI, Meta Horizon for VR, and wearables for AI glasses. [2]

Example: a coaching institute wants WhatsApp replies to its Instagram ad leads. That is one Meta app with three products switched on: WhatsApp, Instagram and the Marketing API.

What can a business build on WhatsApp?

The WhatsApp Cloud API is the official way for software to send and receive WhatsApp messages on a business number. It is the engine behind tools like WATI, AiSensy and Gallabox. Meta's older self-hosted version stopped working in October 2025, so Cloud API is the only route now. [3]

What you can build on it:

  • Instant replies to enquiries. A lead fills a form, and a WhatsApp message with the brochure and a booking link goes out in seconds.
  • Template messages. Pre-approved messages a business can start a chat with: OTPs (authentication), order or fee reminders (utility), and offers (marketing).
  • Chatbots and AI agents that answer questions, qualify a lead and hand over to a person.
  • WhatsApp calls. Meta's Calling API lets customers call the business on WhatsApp; customer-started calls are free. [4]
  • Your own WhatsApp platform for other businesses, if you become a Meta Tech Provider (more on that below).

What does WhatsApp cost now?

Meta charges per delivered message, by message type and the customer's country. That model started on 1 July 2025. [5]

Two newer changes matter for budgets:

  • Replies are no longer free. From 1 October 2026, service messages (your replies inside a customer's chat) are charged per message after 1,000 free each month. [6]
  • Meta's own AI agent is billed by usage. Replies from the Meta Business Agent cost a flat $2 per million tokens, which Meta estimates at roughly 4–5 US cents per message. [5]

One deadline to note if you send offers: marketing templates sent through Cloud API stop working on 13 October 2027. They must move to the separate Marketing Messages API, which uses the same phone number. [7][8]

First-hand note. Taipreneur's own WhatsApp Business account was approved in November 2026, and I am now applying for Meta Tech Provider status. Meta now also offers an MCP server that lets AI coding agents like Claude or Cursor create the account, verify the number and draft templates for you. [9]

What can you automate on Instagram, Facebook and Threads?

The Instagram API works with business and creator accounts. Your app can publish posts and reels, read and reply to comments, handle DMs and pull insights. [10] The popular use is comment-to-DM: someone comments a keyword and gets a private message with the resource. I wrote a full guide on the rules for that: how to automate Instagram comment-to-DM.

The Messenger Platform does the same for Facebook Page chats. The Pages API publishes and moderates Page posts.

The Threads API lets apps publish posts, read profiles and media, moderate replies and get insights. [11] Example: a scheduler that posts the same founder update to Threads and LinkedIn at 9 am.

Facebook Login lets people sign in to your website or app with their Facebook account, so you skip building passwords.

How do Meta's ad APIs bring better leads?

This is where most businesses leave money on the table. Meta's ads learn from the signals you send back. If you only tell Meta "someone filled a form", it finds more form-fillers, including junk ones. If you tell it "this lead booked a visit" or "this lead paid", it looks for more people like them.

  • Lead ads webhook: the moment someone submits a Facebook or Instagram lead form, Meta pings your server with a lead ID, so the lead reaches your CRM and WhatsApp in seconds, not when someone downloads a CSV.
  • Conversions API: your server tells Meta what happened next. It can send web, app, chat and offline events, including events the browser pixel misses, such as phone-call conversions. [12]
  • Conversion Leads: send each lead's status from your CRM, matched by lead ID, and Meta can optimise for leads that become customers. [13]
  • Marketing API: create campaigns, change budgets and pull reports from your own dashboard. The current version is v27.0, released 4 November 2026. [14]

I built a version of this for a group of colleges: Meta Conversions API events from enquiry and admission stages, plus a WhatsApp bot answering parents. The code is the easy half; the loop only works if someone updates lead status in step 4.

What is new in AI, glasses and VR?

These are real platforms in 2026, but I have not built on them yet, so this section only reports what Meta announced.

  • AI models. At Connect 2026 Meta said its Meta Model API is generally available, serving its Muse models, and released Muse Glimmer, an open-weights model small enough for one GPU or a Mac Mini. Llama documentation is still live. [15]
  • Meta AI connectors (preview). Connect your service through an API or MCP server so people can use it inside Meta AI without installing an app. [15]
  • AI glasses. The Wearables Device Access Toolkit connects an iOS or Android app to Meta's AI glasses: camera, microphone and speakers. Example: a site-visit app that lets an agent snap property photos hands-free. Meta Ray-Ban Display also runs web apps. [16][15]
  • VR. Horizon SDKs for Unity, Unreal, Android and web, with new Meta VR Glasses hardware due in spring 2027. Horizon Create and Horizon Studio aim to build games from a description, no engine needed. [15]

How do companies make money with Meta's APIs?

Meta gives the APIs away and charges for messages and ads. Everyone else earns by building something on top that a business will pay for. Five models show up again and again.

1. WhatsApp platforms: subscription plus message fees

AiSensy (India) sells a WhatsApp marketing and support dashboard built on Cloud API. Its founder says it serves 70,000 to 75,000+ businesses; one outside estimate puts its 2025 revenue near $8.9 million, which the company has not confirmed. [18] Its pricing page passes on Meta's Indian rates, such as ₹1.09 per marketing message. [19]

Wati charges a monthly plan, from ₹2,699 (Growth) to ₹16,799 (Business) billed quarterly, with WhatsApp message charges on top. [20] Gallabox, Interakt and others run the same model. This is the Tech Provider route: you sell the software and the customer pays for messages.

2. Messaging infrastructure: a small fee on every message

Twilio sells the plumbing to developers. It charges $0.005 per WhatsApp message, inbound or outbound, and passes Meta's fees through on top. [21] Half a cent sounds small; across billions of messages it is a business.

3. DM automation tools: subscriptions for creators and brands

Manychat built comment-to-DM and chat automation on the Instagram and Messenger APIs. In April 2025 it raised $140 million, saying it serves more than a million businesses in 170+ countries. [22] The product is simple to explain: turn comments and DMs into leads and sales, for a monthly fee.

4. Marketing agencies: set up tracking, charge for results

Agencies earn by installing the Conversions API and running the ads that benefit from it. Two vendor-published examples, not independently checked:

  • Fashion retailer Ibizamode cut its cost per purchase by 20% after switching on the Conversions API through Spotler. [23]
  • Agency Jellyfish reports a client's return on ad spend rose 122% and cost per purchase fell 50% after a Conversions API project. [24]

Treat such numbers as best cases. They show why clients pay for the work, not what every account will get.

5. Freelancers and small agencies: build once, charge monthly

This is Taipreneur's model. A typical engagement: a one-time setup fee to connect lead ads, the CRM and WhatsApp, then a monthly fee to run and improve it. Once the same setup works for three clients in one industry, it becomes a product you can sell to the fourth without rebuilding it.

What approvals do you need before going live?

Testing is open: you can call most APIs with your own accounts in development mode. Going live with real customers needs three things.

  1. Business verification. Meta checks your business documents against your business portfolio.
  2. App Review. For each permission, you explain how you use it and record a short screencast. You also need a public privacy policy, terms and a data-deletion page. Taipreneur added these to its site this month for exactly this reason.
  3. Tech Provider status, only if you serve other businesses' accounts. Meta retired the separate access verification step for Tech Providers in October 2026; business verification and App Review are still required. [17]

Also plan for upgrades. Meta releases a new Graph API version every few months and retires old ones; v21.0 is removed on 21 January 2027. [14] An integration nobody maintains will break.

Frequently asked questions

Is Meta for Developers free?

Creating an app and calling most APIs is free. You pay for what goes through them: WhatsApp messages per delivered message, ads through your ad account, and Meta's AI agent by usage. (Meta, WhatsApp pricing)

Do I need to code?

Not always. Tools like WATI, Manychat or n8n sit on top of these APIs. You need code, or someone who codes, when you want your own logic, your own CRM link or your own product.

What is a Meta Tech Provider?

A business whose app manages other businesses' Meta accounts, such as a WhatsApp platform used by many clients. It needs business verification and App Review. (Social Media Today)

How do WhatsApp platforms like Wati and AiSensy make money?

Mostly a monthly software plan, with WhatsApp message charges on top. Wati, for example, lists plans from ₹2,699 to ₹16,799 a month billed quarterly, plus message charges. (eChai, Wati pricing)

Will marketing messages keep working on Cloud API?

Until 13 October 2027. After that, WhatsApp marketing templates go only through the Marketing Messages API. (PPC Land)

What should you do today?

Pick one leak, not five products. For most businesses that is the gap between a lead arriving and the first reply. Create a Meta app, connect a test WhatsApp number, and make one lead get one useful message within a minute. Add the Conversions API once your team is marking lead outcomes every day.
