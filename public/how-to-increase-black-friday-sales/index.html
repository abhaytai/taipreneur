How to Increase Black Friday Sales: 13 Practical Tips

13 practical Black Friday ideas: build early interest, segment your email list, fix landing pages, and create offers and bundles that stay profitable.

Increasing Black Friday sales starts before the sale goes live. Customers need a reason to pay attention, an offer worth considering and a website that makes buying easy.

From building early interest to creating better packages, here are 13 practical ideas you can use for Black Friday and adapt for Christmas.

1. Build momentum before the sale

Start talking about your upcoming sale before Black Friday arrives. Preview selected products, announce when offers begin and give customers a reason to return.

Create a simple sequence: introduce the sale, reveal some offers, then remind people when it opens. Only use deadlines and limited-stock claims that are real.

2. Warm up your social media and website

Before filling your channels with promotional posts, help customers understand what you sell.

Share product demonstrations, customer questions, useful tips and genuine reviews. On your website, refresh product information and highlight the items you plan to promote. Give people enough information to start choosing before the discounts arrive.

3. Segment your email list and reach existing customers first

Avoid sending the same message to everyone. Separate subscribers who have never purchased, recent customers and customers who have not ordered for a while.

Give each group a relevant message. New subscribers may need an introduction to your products, while existing customers may appreciate early access or an offer that complements a previous purchase. Send promotional emails only to people you have permission to contact.

4. Update relevant keywords and metadata

Review the titles, descriptions and headings on your sale pages. Make them accurately describe the products and offers available.

A specific title such as “Black Friday Offers on Children’s Chess Books” tells visitors more than “Biggest Sale Ever.” Use seasonal terms naturally and update expired offers after the campaign. Changing metadata alone will not guarantee search traffic.

5. Give your website a Black Friday or Christmas theme

Make the sale easy to spot with a seasonal banner, relevant images and clear links to your offers.

Keep the design consistent with your brand. Visitors should quickly understand what is on sale, when the offer ends and where to shop. For Christmas, shift the presentation toward gifting rather than leaving Black Friday banners running.

6. Optimise your landing pages

Send visitors to a page that matches the promotion they clicked.

Show the product, offer price, key benefits and purchase button clearly. Explain delivery charges, timelines and return conditions before checkout. Check the page on mobile and remove unnecessary steps that make buying difficult.

7. Invest in useful creative videos

Create videos that show the product in action. Demonstrate how it works, what comes in the package and who it is useful for.

For a service, explain what the customer receives and show a relevant example. Clear demonstrations can be more useful than expensive production. Add captions so the message remains understandable without sound.

8. Promote your discounts through ads and reels

Use your strongest videos and offer visuals in reels and advertising campaigns.

Keep each promotion focused on one clear message: the product, the offer and the next step. Link to the matching landing page. Start with a controlled ad budget and judge results by purchases and profitability, rather than views alone.

9. Add a relevant free gift

A useful free gift gives customers another reason to consider your offer.

Choose something that complements the main purchase, such as a bookmark with a book or a small accessory with a product. If the gift requires a minimum order value, make that condition clear. Include its cost when checking whether the offer remains profitable.

10. Discount slow-selling items selectively

Review products that have been selling slowly and consider a deeper discount where the numbers allow it.

Check why they are not selling first. Poor photos or unclear descriptions may need fixing alongside the price. Choose discounts based on stock, costs and remaining margin, rather than applying the same heavy reduction to every slow-moving item.

11. Create a buy-one-get-one offer

A buy-one-get-one offer can suit products customers use regularly, share or buy as gifts.

Clearly explain which items qualify and whether the second item is free or discounted. Calculate the cost of supplying both items, including delivery, before setting the offer. Make the terms easy to understand without requiring customers to read through complicated conditions.

12. Build value-driven packages

Combine related products into a package that solves a complete need.

For example, a beginner’s chess bundle could include a learning book, a board and practice materials. Show what is included, the genuine separate-item total and the package price. Each item should contribute something useful to the bundle.

13. Offer more services at the same price

For service businesses, consider adding a useful extra while keeping the main price unchanged.

A website package might include an additional landing-page review, while a marketing package could include a seasonal creative set. Define the extra deliverable clearly and check that you have the capacity to provide it. Avoid vague promises such as unlimited support.

Start with the ideas that fit your customers and your margins. A clear offer, relevant promotion and straightforward buying experience give your Black Friday campaign a stronger foundation. For Christmas, adapt the same approach around gifting and realistic delivery deadlines.
